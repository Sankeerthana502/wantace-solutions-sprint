## 3. Operational Tools & Tracking Stack Today
* **Primary Ledger:** 200-page Hardbound Diary ("Lal Register") maintained on Harish ji’s desk. Contains date, party name, rough specs, advance, and balance due.
* **Floor Communication:** Three informal WhatsApp groups (*"Laser Cutting"*, *"Assembly Floor"*, *"Dispatch Updates"*). Voice notes and photos are primary.
* **Component Tracking:** Hand-scrawled chalk numbers on raw sheet metal faces and torn paper slips held by metal binder clips.
* **Accounting:** Tally ERP 9 maintained by a visiting accountant twice weekly (used solely for tax/GST invoices, completely disconnected from daily production).

---

## 4. The Core Operational Bottleneck: "The External Polishing Blindspot"
* **The Problem:** Sheet metal blanks are cut and bent in-house, then bundled and dispatched via mini-tempo to third-party electro-polishing vendors in Naraina. When buffed parts return 48–72 hours later, components from 3 different customer orders arrive mixed together in the same tempo.
* **Why It Fails:** The factory has no piece-level serial tracking. Workers spend 45 to 60 minutes cross-referencing returned bent panels against loose paper chits. If even 1 shelf panel is missing or scratched at the polishing vendor, the entire assembly stalls at final dispatch.

---

## 5. Quantified Business Impact
* **Time Wasted:** Harish ji spends **6 to 8 hours every week** physically walking between the assembly floor and the gate scale, calling the buffer to locate missing panels.
* **WIP Inventory Lockup:** At any given time, **₹2,80,000 to ₹3,50,000** worth of semi-finished kitchen counters sit parked on the floor, unable to be invoiced because a single component is stuck at the external vendor.
* **Scrap / Rework Losses:** The owner estimates an outright cash loss of **₹35,000 to ₹50,000 per month** due to scratched/dented parts during unmonitored vendor transport and duplicated re-cutting when parts are presumed lost.
